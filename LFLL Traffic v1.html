LFLL · Lyon–Saint-Exupéry ATC Simulator

Parallel runways 17R/35L (4000 m, west side) and 17L/35R (2670 m, east side), about 350 m apart. One runway for arrivals, the other for departures. All traffic is airline IFR traffic.

OBJECTIVE

  • Arrivals (blue aircraft) enter at the first fix of a real LFLL STAR (9N / 9S), which continues into its RNAV initial approach (3N / 3S). Descend them, keep them on the procedure or vector them into the ILS cone, then clear them for the ILS. Their label text turns yellow once they are established on the localizer.
  • Departures (green aircraft) appear lined up on the departure runway and follow a SID. Clear them to climb to FL150 or above before the last fix of their SID, where they are handed off (BELUS stays at or below FL120, so it needs FL110). Above 10000 ft they accelerate automatically to 320 kt. The STOP SPAWN button above the departure strips (or D) stops new departures from appearing.
  • Keep 3 NM / 1000 ft separation. Amber means predicted conflict, red means loss of separation. TCAS TA/RA alerts are modelled: on an RA one aircraft climbs 1000 ft and the other descends 1000 ft.

CONTROLS

  • Mouse wheel zooms at the cursor. Drag pans. Click an aircraft or its strip to select it. Double-click centres it. Hover an aircraft for extra label info.
  • The panel at the bottom sets altitude, heading, speed, direct-to, holds, ILS clearance, go-around and take-off clearance. Pilots read back your instructions by voice (🔊 toggles).
  • Keys: ←→ heading ±5 (Shift ±10) · ↑↓ altitude ±1000 (Shift ±100) · WS speed ±10 · I clear ILS · G go around · H hold at present position · N resume nav / exit hold · T take-off · D stop / resume departure spawn · A stop / resume arrival spawn · L show / hide LL points · C centre · Space pause · 123 speed · Esc deselect
  • The compass (bottom-left) shows the selected aircraft's heading, its cleared heading (green bug), the ILS course (purple) and the bearing to your cursor (yellow). Move the cursor on the map to read bearing and distance.

ILS HOW-TO

  • The purple cone (±15°, 28 NM) is the localizer capture zone. Published fixes on the extended centreline: the IF (INGUV 35R, NEGPU 35L, EKMEP 17L, ARPOB 17R, 3000–4500 ft) and the FAF, about 7 NM out. Either leave the aircraft on its procedure or descend to about 3000 ft and fly a heading that crosses into the cone at 20–40° to the course. Then press CLEARED ILS.
  • Once on the localizer the aircraft follows a smooth intercept, slows along a distance-based speed profile, limits its descent rate while it is still fast, and captures the glideslope. Aircraft that cannot capture the glideslope by 2 NM go around. You can order a GO AROUND at any time on final.
  • Below 10000 ft the speed limit is 250 kt. The base minimum vectoring altitude is 3000 ft.

MINIMUM ALTITUDES

  • The MVA layer shows minimum-altitude sectors: soft shaded areas that follow the main ridges of the Alps, Jura and Massif Central, with one altitude label each, cooler to warmer as the minimum rises. Unshaded airspace is 3000 ft. The altitude controls will not clear an aircraft below the sector it is in. Move the cursor over the map to read the MVA at that point. Toggle the layer in the settings panel.

HOLDING

  • Holds exist at the four STAR end fixes ARBON, GOMET, TALAR and RIPTU (the patterns are not drawn on the map). Pick a fix in the HOLD row, optionally change inbound course / turn direction, then press HOLD. The aircraft flies a direct, parallel or teardrop entry. HOLD PPOS holds at the present position. Any new heading or EXIT leaves the hold.
Entertainment simulator. Fixes, SIDs, STARs and initial approaches come from the eAIP France, AIRAC 29 OCT 2026, except the two north arrivals ARFOZ and AMORO, which are invented routes between real points. Altitude constraints appear on the selected aircraft's route but are not flown automatically. Holds and minimum altitudes are illustrative. Must not be used for real navigation.
